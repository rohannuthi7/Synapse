Synapse
=======

Identify one digit, 0 through 9, from an n×n grid of light cells. Each cell turns light into a single bit. Each digit owns a weight for every cell. The digit with the largest weighted sum of the bits is the prediction.


The cell
--------

One analog circuit per cell. The parts are a light-dependent resistor, a fixed resistor, a capacitor, a reset switch, and a comparator.

    Vdd
     |
    LDR          resistance falls when light hits it
     |
     +-- VL -- C to ground
     |         Rb to ground
     |         Reset switch to ground
     |
    Rser
     |
     +-------- comparator + input
     |              comparator - input is VTH
     Rh             comparator output is Vo
     |
    Vo

LDR and Rb are a divider. Their midpoint is VL.

    VL settles toward  Vdd * Rb / (Rb + LDR)

Bright light makes LDR small, so VL rises toward Vdd. Darkness makes LDR large, so VL falls toward 0. That is the opposite of a series resistor in front of a stiff voltage source. A stiff source charges every capacitor to the same final voltage, and the resistor only changes the speed. Here the light changes the final voltage, which is what makes a lit cell different from a dark one.

C sits on VL. It holds the voltage and slows the edges, so a brief shadow or a flicker does not flip the bit by itself. Reset is a switch across C. Before each frame it shorts VL to ground, so a cell that was 1 on the previous digit can become 0.

The comparator watches VL against a threshold VTH. Vo is a logic 1 when VL is above VTH and a logic 0 when it is below. The capacitor node drives the comparator input that sends Vo high. VTH drives the other input.

Rh is a resistor from Vo back to the comparator's signal input, on the far side of a small series resistor Rser. The capacitor itself is a low impedance, so feedback tied directly across C does nothing useful. Rser gives the feedback a place to shift the effective threshold. When Vo is 1, the bar to fall back to 0 is a little lower than the bar that was required to become 1. The bit does not chatter when VL sits on the threshold.


What the bit means
------------------

A cell is not measuring how bright the ink is, past the threshold. Everything above VTH is 1. Everything below is 0. Gray becomes whichever side of the threshold it landed on.

A correct 1 requires all of these at the sample moment:

    the cell is actually lit
    LDR has fallen enough that VL can exceed VTH
    enough time has passed for C to follow VL
    Reset is open

A correct 0 requires the complementary case: darkness, or light too weak to reach VTH, with C discharged from the previous frame. Ambient light that lifts every LDR partway will push dark cells over VTH and paint 1s where the digit has no stroke.


The grid
--------

n by n of those bits, captured on the same frame, is the image. A practical discrete build is 5 by 5. That is 25 analog cells. 8 by 8 is 64 cells and is mostly wiring. The bits are read together after Reset has been released and C has had time to settle.

The digit is supposed to fill the grid, stay centered, and be much brighter than an empty cell. The grid does not search a page for a digit. It classifies the one pattern already sitting on the cells.

Number the cells in row-major order. A 5 by 5 frame is a vector x of 25 bits. x_i is 1 if cell i is lit.


The weights
-----------

Each digit d from 0 to 9 has a weight vector w_d with one weight per cell. The score is the dot product

    score(d) = sum over i of  w_d,i * x_i

The predicted digit is the d with the largest score. Ties are reported as ties, not silently awarded to the smaller index.

Weights come from example frames, then they are frozen. A direct choice is the average bit vector of the examples of that digit, optionally shifted so a cell that is lit for every digit carries little weight. A cell that is lit only for an 8 should carry a large positive weight on the 8 and a small or negative weight on the digits that are dark there. Negative weights are allowed. They let a lit cell vote against a digit.

The weights are not inside the analog cell. LDR is the sensor. The weight is a number applied after Vo exists. Mixing those two jobs, by trying to make the resistor both the light sensor and the digit weight, makes the threshold depend on which digit is being tested. The circuit senses first. The sum runs after the bits exist.

Worked on three cells, not a full grid. Suppose the bits are

    x = [1, 0, 1]

and two digits own

    w_0 = [ 1,  1, -1 ]
    w_1 = [ 1, -1,  1 ]

    score(0) = 1*1 + 0*1 + 1*(-1) = 0
    score(1) = 1*1 + 0*(-1) + 1*1 = 2

The prediction is 1. The middle cell is dark, so its weights never enter the sum. The third cell is lit, which counts for digit 1 and against digit 0.


What a frame is
---------------

    1. Close Reset. Every capacitor is forced to ground. Every Vo that was high is released once VL falls through the hysteresis band.
    2. Open Reset. Light on each LDR starts charging that cell's C.
    3. Wait a fixed settle time, the same every frame.
    4. Read all n×n comparator outputs into the bit vector x.
    5. Compute the ten scores. Emit x and the winning digit.

The settle time is part of the design. Too short and a lit cell is still below VTH. Too long is harmless for this divider, because VL stops at the divider voltage rather than climbing forever. The time has to be long enough for the dimmest stroke that should still count as lit.


Where it works
--------------

One digit, one font or one stroke style, centered, high contrast, grid size matched to the digit. Under those conditions the same cells light for the same digit, the scores separate, and the winner repeats.

It fails when the lit cells move off the weights:

    the digit is shifted by a cell
    the digit is larger or smaller than the examples
    the stroke is much thicker or thinner
    the digit is handwritten in a style the weights never saw
    ambient light turns empty cells on
    a threshold differs from cell to cell, so the same brightness is 1 on one cell and 0 on its neighbor

A 5 by 5 binary picture is also a coarse image. An 8 and a 0 differ by a few middle cells. One flipped bit in that region can swap the winner. That is a property of the representation, not a tuning error.


What is built
-------------

The analog grid, the reset line shared across cells, and the readout of the bits.

A fixed table of ten weight vectors.

A score step that produces the ten numbers and the argmax.

Evidence that a centered high-contrast digit of each class produces its own largest score, and that a shifted or washed-out frame does not have to. Both outcomes belong in the result. A demo that only shows the ten clean digits is incomplete.

The earlier spiking-network study is not this project. This one has no spikes, no router, and no on-chip learning. The capacitor is only the memory of one cell's brightness for one frame.
